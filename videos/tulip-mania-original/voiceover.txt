In 1637, one tulip bulb sold for 10,000 guilders.

A skilled worker earned about 300 a year.

Traders bid in taverns for bulbs still underground.

They called it the wind trade. No flowers changed hands. Only paper promises.

February 1637: an auction in Haarlem found no buyers.

Within days, tulip prices collapsed.

Haarlem let buyers walk away for 3.5 percent of the price.

But a whole country ruined? Historians say no.

Only a handful of traders hit real trouble.

The ruin story came from satire, retold in an 1841 book.

The real bubble was the story.
