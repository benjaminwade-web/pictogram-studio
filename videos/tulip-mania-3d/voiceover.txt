In 1637, one tulip bulb sold for 10,000 guilders.
A skilled craftsman earned about 300 a year.
So traders packed into taverns, bidding on bulbs still buried in the ground.
They called it the wind trade. No flowers changed hands. Just paper promises.
Then, in February, an auction in Haarlem drew no buyers at all.
Within days, prices collapsed.
Haarlem let buyers walk away for 3.5 percent of the price.
But did it ruin the Dutch economy? Historians say no.
Only a handful of traders got into real trouble.
The ruin story came mostly from satire, retold in a famous 1841 book.
The real bubble was the story.
