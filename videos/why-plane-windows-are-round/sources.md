# Sources

- The de Havilland Comet flew its first passenger service on May 2, 1952, with square cabin windows. BOAC Flight 781 (January 10, 1954) and South African Airways Flight 201 (April 8, 1954) broke up in flight. Investigators blamed metal fatigue from repeated pressurising, with cracks starting at the square corners of cutouts (Jason Lefkowitz, Why airplane windows have round corners (2016)): https://jasonlefkowitz.net/2016/09/why-airplane-windows-have-round-corners/
- The metal around the corners took two to three times more stress than the rest of the cabin. Later aircraft got rounded or oval windows (Yahoo News UK, The alarming reason plane windows were changed from square to round): https://uk.news.yahoo.com/alarming-reason-plane-windows-were-111101276.html
