# Style notes

## The seven styles and when to use each

A style changes the colors, font, background texture, how figures are drawn, how captions are highlighted and how scenes change. The words and scenes stay the same, so one script can be tried in any look. `out/style-reel_9x16.mp4` shows all seven.

| Style | What it looks like | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Neon terminal | Black, glowing green, dotted grid, fast whip between scenes | Money, tech, "secret strategy" stories. The original example look. |
| Whiteboard | Light grid paper, outlined figures, red underlines, rounded friendly font | How-to, study tips, explaining a process step by step |
| Blueprint | Deep blue, thin yellow and white lines, typewriter font | How things work, engineering, science, business models |
| Newsroom | Near black, big condensed capitals, red boxes, hard cuts | News, breaking stories, numbers that should feel urgent |
| Arcade CRT | Purple with scan lines and strong cyan glow | Gaming, internet culture, playful rankings |
| Magazine | Warm dark paper, serif type, orange underlines, soft fades | History, psychology, calmer story-driven pieces |
| Sticker pop | Bright yellow, flat blue and black, boxed highlights, hard cuts | Self-improvement, lifestyle, anything aimed at a wide audience |

Tips: keep one style per series so people recognise it, and change style between series so the feed does not look like one template. Light styles (Whiteboard, Sticker pop) stand out in a feed that is mostly dark video.

# Style breakdown of the original example

Source: 61.3 second animated explainer, 16:9 picture, 30 fps, 16 story beats.

## The system

- **Canvas**: near-black background, faint dotted grid, dark vignette, one dashed "ground" line near the bottom that never moves.
- **Two kinds of people**: everyone is a white pictogram (round head, bell-shaped body). The "smart" ones are green and wear round glasses. That one color difference carries the whole story.
- **Props are outlines**: chests, pedestals, panels, pins and the clock are thin white line drawings. Only people, counters and the prize are solid.
- **One accent color**: green is used for the smart character, the highlighted word, counters, progress and the prize. Nothing else is colored.
- **Glow**: every shape has a soft bloom, with a larger green halo behind whatever matters in the scene.
- **Type**: bold grotesque in capitals with wide letter spacing. A tiny grey setup line on top, then the main line under it with exactly one word or phrase in green.
- **Micro labels**: small outlined pills ("ADDICTION", "QUIT", "ONE LESS") and tiny spaced captions ("POSSIBLE SPOTS") label things like a dashboard.

## The motion

- A scene lasts 2 to 6 seconds. The setup line fades in first, the punch line lands 1 to 2 seconds later, and the picture changes on the punch line.
- Things pop in with a slight overshoot, one after another, never all at once.
- Numbers roll like a slot machine. Bars and curves draw themselves.
- Scenes change with a fast sideways whip and motion blur. The ground line stays still, which makes it feel like one continuous world.
- A slow push-in runs through every scene so nothing is ever fully still.

## The script pattern

1. Hook: "Some of the smartest people are quietly chasing X."
2. Reframe: "Not because A, but because they understand something most people don't."
3. Principle with a picture (the rarity curve, the pedestals).
4. What most people do (open, see nothing, leave).
5. What smart people do differently (cross off the grid, keep a log).
6. Social friction (friends call it an addiction, the crowd shouts opinions).
7. The near miss (most people quit one short).
8. Payoff number and the line that ties back to the hook.
9. Follow card with a joke.

Each line is 12 words or fewer, and the highlighted word is the one that would still make sense on its own.

## How the recreation maps to it

Every beat above is a reusable scene type in Pictogram Studio, timed to the original to within about a quarter second. Differences you may notice: the original has a few hand-animated morphs between scenes (for example the curve folding into a pedestal) and a slightly wandering camera, which the recreation replaces with the whip transition and a steady push-in.
